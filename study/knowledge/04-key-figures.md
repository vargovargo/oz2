## The tax incentive (P.L. 119-21, amending IRC §§ 1400Z-1, 1400Z-2)

| Mechanic | Standard QOF | QROF (rural) |
|---|---|---|
| Window to invest a realized gain | 180 days | 180 days |
| Deferral runs until | earlier of sale or the 5th anniversary | same |
| Basis step-up at year 5 | **10%** | **30%** |
| Appreciation exclusion | 100% at a 10-year hold | 100% at a 10-year hold |
| Substantial improvement threshold | **100%** of adjusted basis, within 30 months | **50%** |
| QOF asset test | 90% in qualified opportunity zone property | same |

The 90% asset test is IRC § 1400Z-2(d)(1). It is measured as the **average** of two testing
dates — the last day of the fund's first six-month period and the last day of its taxable
year — not continuously. Funds self-certify and report compliance on Form 8996.

**QROF qualification is stricter than "holds rural property."** A QROF must satisfy the 90%
test with QOZ business property used in a zone comprised **entirely of a rural area**, or
with QOZ stock or partnership interests in a qualifying rural QOZ business. A fund mixing
rural and non-rural assets does not get the 30% step-up. "Rural area" per Notice 2025-50
§ 4.01: any area other than a city or town over 50,000 population and any urbanized area
contiguous and adjacent to it.

## CRA overlap (FFIEC Census Flat File, 2025 exam year)

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| OZ-eligible tracts carrying a CRA designation | **18,968 — 74.9%** |
| Of those, Track 1 (LMI, under 80% of area MFI) | **17,378** (68.6% of all eligible) |
| Track 2 only (distressed/underserved non-metro middle-income) | **1,590** (6.3%) |
| Track 1 split | 5,260 Low Income (<50%), 12,118 Moderate (50–80%) |
| Non-rural tracts CRA-eligible | 77.4% |
| Rural tracts CRA-eligible | 69.7% |
| **Rural tracts with QROF bonus and no CRA flag** | **2,525** (30.3% of the rural set) |
| "Sweet spot" — CRA-eligible *and* rural/QROF | **5,809** |
| Tracts in USDA persistent-poverty counties | 3,122 (12.3%) |
| Tracts overlapping tribal areas or >25% AIAN population | 1,067 (4.2%) |

Track 2 criteria: a non-metro tract at 80–120% of the statewide non-metro median MFI that
meets at least one of — unemployment ≥ 1.5× the national average; poverty ≥ 20%;
population loss ≥ 10% between the 2010 and 2020 Censuses (or net migration loss ≥ 5%); or
a USDA urban influence code of 7, 10, 11, or 12.

Regulatory state: the 2023 CRA Final Rule was **rescinded by the banking agencies in 2025**
following a court injunction. The pre-2023 framework is in effect. (Note: the June 2026
brief at `docs/cra-oz-overlap-brief.md` describes it as "under legal challenge" — that
wording predates the rescission. Use the 2025 rescission.)

States with the largest non-CRA gap: Puerto Rico (57.6% not eligible), Oregon (40.6%),
West Virginia (34.8% — 72 of 207 tracts), Arkansas (26.8%).

## OZ 1.0 outcomes (Coyne & Johnson, Treasury OTA WP-128, June 2026, unless noted)

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Cumulative qualified investment through TY2024 | **$112 billion** |
| Total QOF assets | $116 billion across roughly 12,800 funds |
| Designated OZs receiving any investment, by end of 2024 | **77%** (up from 48% through 2020) |
| Tracts still at zero | 23% — just over 1,800 |
| Tracts receiving under $1M | a further 34% |
| Share held by the top 20% of funded tracts | about **90%** |
| Rural share of OZs (OBBBA definition) | **37.9%** |
| Rural share of investment | **16.1%** (up from 12.6% in 2020) |
| Average per funded rural OZ | **$7.3M** vs **$23.3M** non-rural |
| Taxpayers reporting deferred gains, 2024 | roughly **41,000** |
| Typical individual filer AGI | **$738,000** (vs $54,000 for the typical Form 1040 filer) |
| Individual share of Forms 8997 | about 85%; over 60% of deferred gains |
| Deferred gains on Form 8997, 2024 | $75B — down from ~81% of QOF assets in 2020 to ~65% in 2024 |

Distress ranking (Gelfond–Looney index): the most distressed deciles of OZs received well
below an equal share of investment; the least-distressed decile received more than double
its share. Through 2024, 35% of investment went to the highest-income quintile of OZ
tracts within each state, against 17% to the lowest (WP-128 Table 17).

**Kennedy & Wheeler (2022)**, tax years 2018–2020: only 1,347 of 8,764 designated tracts
received any investment in 2019; the top 1% of tracts absorbed 42% of all capital and the
top 5% took 78%. By 2020, 3,242 tracts were funded but 63% still received nothing from
electronic filers. Roughly 21,000 individual investors in 2020, median AGI approximately
**$741,000** against a national median household income of $69,000.

**JCT (May 2024)**, through TY2022: $84.7B cumulative, $77.2B matched to tracts; 8.5% of
tract-matched investment reached rural areas on JCT's narrower non-metro measure; 94% of
tracts in the top investment decile were metropolitan.

## OZ 2.0 reporting

| Provision | What it does |
|---|---|
| **IRC § 6039K** | QOFs file annual information returns — total assets, QOZ property, employment, NAICS codes, housing units. Effective for tax years beginning after December 31, 2026. |
| **IRC § 6039L** | QOZBs furnish corresponding data to the QOFs holding them. |
| **IRC § 6726** | Penalties: $500/day standard, $2,500/day for intentional disregard, capped by fund size. |
| Treasury public reports | Aggregate QOF activity published at years 6 and 11 post-enactment. |

Under OZ 1.0, QOFs self-certified on Form 8996 with no enforceable penalty for non-filing
and no obligation to report community outcomes.
